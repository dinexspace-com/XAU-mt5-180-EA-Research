# Research — EA-048: 10-Bar Donchian Breakout

## Research Objective

EA-048 investigates whether a simple Donchian Channel breakout can provide a viable systematic trend-following strategy for XAUUSD.

The specific implementation being tested uses a short 10-bar lookback and is evaluated on MetaTrader 5 using real-tick historical data.

The purpose of this research is not to assume that the strategy is profitable, but to:

1. Translate the Donchian breakout concept into explicit mechanical rules.
2. Implement those rules as an MT5 Expert Advisor.
3. Backtest the implementation on XAUUSD.
4. Record both positive and negative results.
5. Use the baseline result as evidence for deciding whether further variants deserve testing.

---

## Strategy Background

The Donchian Channel is a classic trend-following concept associated with Richard Donchian.

For a lookback period `N`:

Upper Channel:

    Highest High of the previous N bars

Lower Channel:

    Lowest Low of the previous N bars

The basic trend-following hypothesis is:

> A breakout beyond a recent price extreme may indicate the beginning or continuation of a directional trend.

Rather than attempting to predict tops and bottoms, a breakout system reacts to price reaching new local extremes.

The classical Donchian Four-Week Rule used a substantially longer horizon, commonly represented by approximately 20 trading days.

Later trend-following systems, including the Turtle framework, also used channel breakouts with different entry and exit horizons.

EA-048 should therefore be considered an experimental short-horizon adaptation of the broader Donchian breakout concept rather than an implementation of the original Four-Week Rule.

## EA-048 Research Variant

EA-048 tests:

    Donchian Lookback = 10 bars

The strategy evaluates the previous 10 completed bars.

### Long Entry

Open a BUY position when:

    Current Close > Highest High of previous 10 bars

### Short Entry

Open a SELL position when:

    Current Close < Lowest Low of previous 10 bars

Only one position associated with the EA is maintained at a time for the current symbol.

## Research Hypothesis

The initial hypothesis is:

> A 10-bar price breakout may capture sufficiently strong short-term directional movement in XAUUSD to compensate for losses generated by false breakouts.

This hypothesis requires empirical testing.

A breakout strategy can naturally experience a relatively low win rate because many breakouts fail before a smaller number of sustained trends generate larger winning trades.

Therefore, win rate alone is not sufficient to evaluate the strategy.

Important measurements include:

- Net Profit
- Profit Factor
- Expected Payoff
- Maximum Drawdown
- Recovery Factor
- Sharpe Ratio
- Average Winner / Average Loser
- Trade Count
- Consecutive Losses
- Stability of the equity curve

## Initial Finding

The baseline hypothesis is **not supported by this test configuration**.

The EA generated:

    Profit Factor = 0.98
    Net Profit    = -$30.84

Therefore, gross profits were insufficient to compensate for gross losses during the tested sample.

The system nevertheless produced a larger average winning trade than average losing trade:

    Average Winner = $4.16
    Average Loser  = -$2.90

but the winning frequency of 40.51% was not sufficient to produce positive expectancy under this configuration.

The result is therefore retained as a negative baseline rather than discarded.

---

## Interpretation

This experiment does NOT demonstrate that the Donchian breakout concept itself is invalid.

It demonstrates only that:

> EA-048 with the tested 10-bar configuration, M1 timeframe, XAUUSD.PRO data, specified risk-management rules, and tested historical period did not produce a profitable result.

Changing the lookback period, timeframe, exit mechanism, volatility regime, spread assumptions, or risk-management method creates a different experiment and must be tested separately.

Results from those variants must not be retroactively attributed to this baseline.

## Research Principle

Negative results are part of the research record.

A strategy should not be modified repeatedly until a profitable historical result appears and then presented without its previous failures.

Future EA-048 experiments should therefore preserve:

1. The tested hypothesis.
2. Parameter configuration.
3. Historical period.
4. Raw Strategy Tester report.
5. Result.
6. PASS/FAIL decision.

This allows later variants to be compared against the original baseline without hiding unsuccessful experiments.
